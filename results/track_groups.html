Track types and who does well there

Circuits grouped by how the car is driven on them, measured from OpenF1 telemetry of the three fastest practice laps before the team lock (2023-25 practice for existing circuits; 2026 practice for Madrid and Kuala Lumpur), plus circuit facts from Wikipedia. No race results were used to form the groups. Performance below is 2026 official F1 Fantasy points per weekend, counting only weekends the driver raced.

Both axes are standardised within each season (0 = an average circuit that year), so the faster 2026 cars do not shift the map. Hover a circuit for its raw numbers.

Who over- or under-performs their own average

Points per weekend at each track type minus the driver's 2026 average. The last columns test flowing against stop-start: the difference and its standard error. Only Gasly and Bortoleto clear two standard errors (both better at flowing tracks), and with 22 drivers about one such result is expected by chance, so treat these as tendencies, not proven specialities. Monaco is a single weekend.

Driver only: compared with the teammate (same car, same weekend)

To take the car out, each driver is measured against his teammate: fantasy points minus the teammate's, and qualifying places gained on him. A driver who suits a track type should beat his teammate by more there. With the car removed, no driver's points edge differs clearly between flowing and stop-start tracks; in qualifying only Stroll against Alonso does (Stroll gains on Alonso at flowing tracks, Alonso is relatively better at stop-start ones). Most of the type effects in the table above were the car: for example, Gasly's flowing-track edge disappears against Colapinto, so it was the Alpine. Teammate pairs mirror each other.
